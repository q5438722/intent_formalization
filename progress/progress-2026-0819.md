# Weekly Progress: Rust Standard Library Specifications and Proofs

Snapshot: 2026-08-19 02:28 UTC

## 1. Reducing `external_body` / trust boundaries

This week, we work on Rust 1.96 `core::slice` and `alloc::vec`, and continued replacing or lowering
coarse-grained `#[verifier::external_body]` boundaries with Verus proofs.

### Scope and counting methodology

The proof scope contains **180 functions**:

- 144 generated specifications and 36 exact existing-vstd specifications;
- 132 Slice functions and 48 Vec functions.

Note:
`existing-vstd` only means that the specification comes from Verus vstd. It does
not mean that the proof of the Rust implementation requires no
`external_body`. Allocator, raw-pointer, callback, and mutable-frame semantics
may still require additional boundaries.

### Combined 180-proof surface (initial -> current)

| By Module | Proofs with `external_body` | `external_body` helpers (not deduplicated) | `external_body` helpers (deduplicated) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Slice | 85 -> 77 | 219 -> 149 | 156 -> 113 |
| Vec | 46 -> 31 | 173 -> 70 | 94 -> 49 |
| **Total** | **131 -> 108** | **392 -> 219** | **250 -> 162** |

---

| By Specification source | Proofs with `external_body` | `external_body` helpers (not deduplicated) | `external_body` helpers (deduplicated) |

Duplication comes from helper functions used by more than one API function,
e.g., `rust_1_96_slice_as_ptr_cast`.


Compared with the initial state:

- the number of functions whose proofs contain `external_body` decreased from 131 to **108**;
- the number of functions whose proofs do not contain `external_body` increased from 49 to **72**;
- Number of unique `external_body` decreased by **88 (35%)**.

Note:
- 72 is the count of proofs without `external_body`;
- some of them still depend on uninterpreted specifications, Ghost
  representations, or source-to-model correspondence;
- under the stricter semantic criterion, only 7 require no additional
  implementation/dependency boundary.

## 2. Representative boundary reductions

### 2.1 Pure arithmetic

- `exact_div`:
  - Before: the entire division helper was an `external_body`.
  - After: the proof executes the real integer division and uses `div_mod` and
    multiplication commutativity to prove the quotient and exact-product
    properties.

### 2.2 RawVec fields and pointer accessors

- Before, the proof trusted the complete `as_ptr` / `as_mut_ptr` accessor as an
  `external_body`.
- After, trust is now limited to the fundamental properties of the returned pointer:
  - whether the pointer refers to a real, live allocation;
  - provenance, alignment, and the initialized prefix;
  - allocator ownership and reallocation behavior;
  - whether pointer + length can safely construct `&[T]` / `&mut [T]`;
  - mutable aliasing and final-frame correspondence.
- Similar on `Vec::{as_slice,as_mut_slice}` 

### 2.3 Slice comparison

- `starts_with`, `ends_with`, and the strip-prefix/suffix family:
  - Before: the complete slice prefix/suffix comparison was a trusted helper function.
  - After: the proof executes the real element scan, short-circuit behavior,
    and loop invariant.
  - Only the observation boundary for one `PartialEq::eq` call remains.

### 2.4 Vec growth loops

- `Vec::resize::extend_with` and `resize_with`:
  - Before: the complete private growth helper was an `external_body`.
  - After: the proof executes reserve, the clone/write loop,
    `SetLenOnDrop` length accounting, and the final `set_len`.
  - Only the lower-level allocator/raw-storage effect and the correspondence of
    `Clone` outputs remain `external_body`.

### 2.5 Slice start-pointer consumers

Pointer-range, array/chunk, split, rotate/swap, align, and copy APIs depend on
the two bottom-level APIs `slice::as_ptr` and `slice::as_mut_ptr`. Their proofs
now reuse the trust boundaries of these two APIs instead of duplicating a
target-local start-pointer `external_body` in every consumer. Later
pointer-arithmetic or raw-parts operations may still retain their own lower
boundaries.

## 3. Representative remaining trust boundaries

### 3.1 Raw pointers to slices/references

Representative helpers:

- `rust_1_96_aggregate_raw_ptr_slice`;
- `rust_1_96_aggregate_raw_ptr_mut_slice`;
- `from_raw_parts` / `from_raw_parts_mut`;
- split raw-parts helpers.

Properties that remain trusted or are not yet fully modeled:

- the pointer refers to a real, live allocation;
- provenance is correct;
- the pointer is properly aligned for `T`;
- the entire `0..len` range is initialized;
- the range stays in bounds and does not cause address overflow;
- after creating `&[T]`, the data is not mutated through another path;
- after creating `&mut [T]`, no other alias exists;
- mutations through the mutable slice are reflected correctly in the original
  Vec/Slice.

### 3.2 Allocator and RawVec

Representative operations include `capacity`, `reserve`, `grow`, `shrink`,
`from_raw_parts`, and ownership transfer into raw or boxed storage.

Properties that remain trusted or are not yet fully modeled:

- RawVec owns a real, live allocation through the selected allocator;
- the stored pointer and capacity describe the same allocation;
- the first `len` slots are initialized and the remaining capacity is spare;
- reserve/grow operations preserve the initialized prefix while updating the
  pointer and capacity correctly;
- shrink/reallocation moves or copies elements correctly and invalidates the
  old pointer at the right time;
- allocation failure or panic leaves the Vec in a valid state;
- zero-sized types follow their special capacity/allocation rules;
- ownership transfer does not leak, double-free, or duplicate the allocation.

### 3.3 Mutable aliasing and final frames

Representative operations include `as_mut_slice`, `split_at_mut`,
`get_disjoint_mut`, and APIs returning mutable element or subslice references.

Properties that remain trusted or are not yet fully modeled:

- each returned mutable reference covers exactly the intended logical region;
- the caller receives exclusive permission for that region;
- multiple returned mutable regions do not overlap;
- no other reference or pointer accesses the region during the returned
  lifetime;
- writes through a returned reference update the corresponding indices in the
  parent Vec/Slice;
- elements outside the returned region remain unchanged;
- the final view of the returned mutable reference matches the final state of
  the parent container.

### 3.4 Traits and callbacks

Representative cases include `PartialEq`, `PartialOrd`, `Ord`, `Clone`,
`FnMut`, and `FnOnce`.

Properties that remain trusted or are not yet fully modeled:

- each trait/callback call returns the value recorded by its abstract
  observation;
- calls occur in the same order and number as in the Rust implementation;
- hidden callback state evolves consistently between calls;
- mutations to callback arguments are reflected in their final values;
- values produced by `Clone` satisfy the abstract clone relation;
- callback side effects and panic behavior are not silently ignored.

The traversal, control flow, and aggregation around these calls are proved as
executable code; the retained boundary is limited to each individual
trait/callback invocation.

## 4. Specification completeness

The 180 proof targets consist of:

- 36 exact existing-vstd contracts;
- 144 generated contracts.

The current generated-spec determinism split is:

- strict `UNSAT` (complete): 63;
- `UNKNOWN`: 81;
- unsupported/no-target/semantic-mismatch: 0.

## 5. Next steps

  - work is continuing to lower raw-parts, alias, callback, and allocator
    boundaries.
- continue verifying completeness on the remaining 81 unknown functions
- Extend the work to other modules, such as `core::array`.
