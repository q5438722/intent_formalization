### 2.3 Main root-causes of `UNKNOWN`

We group the remaining `UNKNOWN` cases into two categories:

1. **Hidden or external behavior/state (passed by user).** This includes stateful `FnMut` / `FnOnce` callbacks, `Clone`, `Debug` / `Hash`, and iterators. These cases require an explicit callback trace or iterator view before checking completeness.
2. **Mutable-reference and final-frame comparison.** APIs returning `&mut T` or `&mut [T]` require the checker to compare not only their abstract values, but also pointer-related properties, such as aliasing and ownership correspondence.

Some cases belong to more than one category. For example, `get_or_insert_with`
combines a callback with a mutable-reference result, while
`IntoIter::as_mut_slice` combines iterator private state with mutable
final-frame reasoning.

## 3. Trust boundaries for verification

### 3.1 Reused versus new boundaries

These three modules use 126 unique helper/type boundary symbols. At the
semantic-family level, only **3** boundary families are new.

| Module | #trust boundaries (helper functions) | Reused trust boundaries (from `slice` and `vec`) | New trust boundaries |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `core::array` | 13 | 5 | 0 |
| `core::option` | 67 | 6 | 2 |
| `alloc::string` | 46 | 7 | 1 |
| **Total** | **126** | **8** | **3** |

The three new trust-boundary families are:

- caller-provided `Formatter` / `Hasher` state effects;
- `Pin` projection invariants;
- UTF-16 decoding, lossy conversion, and error relations.

For example, `Option::Debug::fmt` receives a caller-owned `&mut Formatter`, and needs to satisfy:

- the abstract formatter effect corresponds to the real update of the same
  `Formatter`;
- the returned `fmt::Result` matches that effect;
- formatting flags and errors are propagated consistently;
- no additional formatter mutation is omitted.
